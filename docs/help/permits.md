A dangerous job (hot work, enclosed space entry and similar) does not start without a permit that was checked, issued and signed by the right people, and a permit cannot quietly stay open after the work.

## Getting started
1. Office (admin): set up the [Permit types and checklists](/permits/types): the checklist, validity limit, and who may request, issue and close each type.
2. On board, the person doing the job starts a [New permit](/permits/new), picks the type and completes the checklist.
3. The issuer checks and signs it (and the Master approves where the type needs it); the permit is then Active, with gas readings and the entry log as the type requires.
4. When the work is finished the person in charge and the issuer both sign to close it. Watch everything on the [Permit board](/permits).

## Lifecycle
Requested, checklist completed, Issued (and Master approved where the type needs it), Active, Suspended and Resumed, then Completed, Cancelled, Withdrawn or Expired. A permit is valid only inside its time window and only while the last gas reading, where required, is within limits and not older than its repeat interval; otherwise it shows red as not valid.

## During the job
Record gas readings (oxygen, flammable gas, toxic gases) and, for an enclosed space, the entry log on the permit: name, time in and out, and the attendant, so nobody is left inside unnoticed. Hand over between shifts is a recorded event. An extension needs a fresh gas test and the issuer's signature.

## Closing
Completed needs two signatures: the person in charge (work finished, area safe, everyone out) and the issuer. The permit is then locked. An expired permit stays on the board and raises a Worklist task until someone closes it with the status of the work. Permits are never deleted; one raised in error is withdrawn with a reason.

## Linking permits to other records
An expired permit stays on the board and raises a [Worklist](/worklist) task until someone closes it with the status of the work.
